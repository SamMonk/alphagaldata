## Why this site exists

AlphaGalData started the way many alpha-gal stories do: with unexplained symptoms, a long search for answers, and the realization that most of the useful information lived in scattered Facebook groups and forum threads rather than in one organized place.

After developing alpha-gal syndrome from a tick bite, Shawn spent months piecing together what was happening. Delayed GI reactions after meals, seemingly random flare-ups, and doctors who had never heard of the condition made for a frustrating diagnostic journey. Online research eventually pointed to AGS, and a blood test confirmed it. But even with a diagnosis in hand, the practical questions were overwhelming: What can I actually eat? Which medications are safe? How do I explain this to a waiter, a family member, or an ER doctor?

AlphaGalData exists to organize those answers. We combine public health data, plain-language guides, and practical tools so that newly diagnosed patients and their families can skip the months of confusion and get straight to the information that matters.

<h2 id="who-we-are">Who we are</h2>

AlphaGalData is a family project.

- **Shawn Monk** lives with alpha-gal syndrome, runs AlphaGalData, and is responsible for the information on it. Shawn writes or edits the guides, checks medical pages against their sources, and approves everything before it is published.
- **Karrah Monk** is Shawn's wife and patient advocate, and brings the family and caregiver side of AGS to the site: what it takes to cook for, travel with, and speak up for someone with a delayed, unpredictable allergy.

We are not doctors, and nothing on this site is medical advice. What we are is part of the AGS community, living with the same dietary restrictions, medication questions, and daily decisions that every alpha-gal household faces.

## What we offer

- **Ingredient scanner** — Scan a barcode, photograph a label, or paste an ingredient list to flag mammal-derived ingredients that may trigger a reaction.
- **Spread and awareness map** — See how suspected AGS cases and awareness have spread across the U.S., built from CDC's published case data.
- **Recipes** — Recipes we cook at home, checked for alpha-gal triggers, with notes on substitutions and labels to re-check.
- **Medical alert cards and tattoo designs** — Free, downloadable resources to explain your allergy to restaurant staff and medical teams.
- **Guides with sources** — From understanding your diagnosis to medications, dining out, and tick prevention. Each medical guide links to the CDC, peer-reviewed research, or allergy organizations it relies on.
- **Research tracker** — A weekly, plain-language roundup of new alpha-gal studies and clinical trials.

## Our approach

Every medical guide on this site is:

- **Sourced** — We cite the CDC, peer-reviewed studies, and allergy organizations such as ACAAI and AAAAI, with a source list at the bottom of each guide. Where our own experience fills a gap the research hasn't reached yet, we say so.
- **Written from lived experience** — The practical tips come from actually living with AGS: reading every label, calling pharmacies about capsule ingredients, and learning which restaurants can accommodate the allergy.
- **Kept current** — Each guide shows the date it was last checked against its sources. When CDC guidance or the research changes, we update the page.

Read our full [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/) for how we research, write, review, and correct content, including how we use AI tools.

## The alpha-gal community

One of the most important things we learned after diagnosis is that the AGS community itself is an invaluable resource. Facebook groups, Reddit threads, and local support networks are where patients share real-time information about safe products, sympathetic doctors, and restaurant experiences. We encourage every newly diagnosed person to connect with these communities alongside working with a knowledgeable allergist.
