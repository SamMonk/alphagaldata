If a scanner result leaves you with more questions than confidence, that is normal. Alpha-gal safety decisions often depend on what the label says, what it leaves out, and how the food is prepared. Use this page to turn a `known_risk`, `caution`, or `unknown` result into a practical next step.

## <IconInfo className="text-teal-700"/> Start with the result you got

- **`known_risk / avoid`** means the ingredient is a direct red flag. The safest next step is to stop trying to justify the product and move to a better alternative.
- **`caution / verify_before_using`** means the ingredient name or source can vary. Slow down, read the full label, and verify details before using it.
- **`unknown / research_before_buying`** means the scanner could not confidently classify the ingredient. Treat that uncertainty as real information, not as a quiet green light.

## <IconInfo className="text-teal-700"/> Label-reading habits that prevent mistakes

Ingredient panels matter more than allergen statements for alpha-gal. FDA allergen callouts can miss mammalian-derived ingredients, so the safest habit is reading the full ingredient list every time.

Focus on these patterns:

- **Direct mammalian ingredients** such as gelatin, lard, tallow, beef fat, pork fat, suet, broth, stock, and meat extracts.
- **Source-dependent ingredients** such as natural flavors, stearic acid, magnesium stearate, mono- and diglycerides, glycerin, enzymes, and emulsifiers that may be plant, microbial, or mammal-sourced depending on the product.
- **Dairy-derived terms** such as whey, casein, lactose, butterfat, or cream if dairy is part of your reaction profile.
- **Formulation drift** when a product was safe before but the ingredient list has changed since the last time you checked it.

If the wording is vague, do not fill in the blanks with optimism. That is the point where `verify_before_using` or `research_before_buying` matters.

## <IconInfo className="text-teal-700"/> Questions worth asking when the label is vague

When a product lands in `caution` or `unknown`, the label often cannot settle the question by itself. A practical follow-up is contacting the manufacturer or checking a better source panel.

Useful questions:

- What is the source of the flavoring, stabilizer, capsule, fat, or emulsifier?
- Has the formulation changed recently?
- Is the product made on shared equipment with mammalian ingredients?
- Is the broth, sauce, frying medium, or shortening plant-based or mammalian-derived?

If the answer stays vague, keep the outcome conservative. A vague answer is not the same thing as a safe answer.

## <IconInfo className="text-teal-700"/> Cross-contamination changes the decision

Even a cleaner ingredient list does not cover every risk. Shared preparation can matter in restaurants, deli counters, bakeries, family kitchens, and packaged foods.

Watch for these situations:

- **Shared grills or flat-tops** where meat fats stay on the cooking surface.
- **Shared fryers** where fries, vegetables, or seafood are cooked in oil that also handles breaded meat or animal-fat items.
- **Broths, sauces, and pan drippings** that are added after cooking and may not be obvious from the front-facing menu description.
- **Cutting boards, utensils, or prep surfaces** that are reused without a clean separation.
- **Bakery and deli environments** where butter, lard, meat residues, or shared toppings create risk that is not obvious on a label.

This is why a product or meal can still need caution even when the ingredient list looks better than expected.

## <IconInfo className="text-teal-700"/> What to do in real shopping and dining situations

### If you are shopping

- Read the full ingredient panel, not just the front-of-package claims.
- Compare brands rather than forcing a questionable product to work.
- Use the [scanner](/scanner) again if you can get a cleaner ingredient panel or more complete text.
- Move to safer alternatives or [recipes](/recipes) when the answer stays murky.

### If you are eating out

- Ask about cooking fats, broth bases, sauces, and shared surfaces directly.
- Use the [dining card guide](/learn/dining-card-guide) when you need a clearer way to explain the restriction in a busy kitchen.
- Treat a restaurant answer as incomplete if it only covers the main ingredient and ignores the prep method.
