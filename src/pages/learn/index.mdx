Alpha-gal syndrome (AGS) is an allergy linked to tick bites that makes people react to meat and other products from mammals, usually hours after eating. Because the reactions are delayed and many doctors are still unfamiliar with it, most people have to learn a lot, fast. These guides are written by [Shawn Monk](/about/#who-we-are), who lives with AGS, and each medical guide is checked against CDC guidance, peer-reviewed research, and allergy organizations, with sources listed at the bottom.

## If you think you might have AGS

1. **[What is alpha-gal syndrome?](/learn/what-is-alpha-gal)** What causes it, why reactions are delayed, how common it is, and whether it can fade.
2. **[Symptoms and diagnosis](/learn/symptoms-and-diagnosis)** The symptoms to watch for, including stomach-only reactions, why diagnosis takes so long, and how the alpha-gal blood test works.
3. **[Testing guide](/testing)** Lab test codes for Labcorp and Quest, how to order, and how to find an allergist who knows AGS.

## If you were just diagnosed

1. **[Alpha-gal 101](/learn/alpha-gal-101)** An orientation for your first months: what to avoid, what is safe, the dairy question, everyday swaps, and building a safety net.

3. **[Label reading and cross-contamination](/learn/label-reading-and-cross-contamination)** The ingredient names to watch, the questions to ask manufacturers, and where shared grills and fryers matter.
