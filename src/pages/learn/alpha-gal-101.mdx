If you have recently been diagnosed with alpha-gal syndrome — or suspect you might have it — this guide covers everything you need to know to get oriented. AGS changes how you eat, how you manage medications, and how you interact with the healthcare system. The learning curve is steep, but the alpha-gal community has been navigating it for years, and the practical knowledge base is deep.

## What is alpha-gal syndrome?

Alpha-gal syndrome (AGS) is a serious, potentially life-threatening allergy linked to tick bites ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/about/index.html)). People with AGS react to galactose-α-1,3-galactose ("alpha-gal"), a sugar molecule made by most mammals but not by people. In the U.S., the lone star tick is most often associated with AGS. Alpha-gal can be present in tick saliva and may enter the body during a bite ([ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/food/meat/)); the immune system may then make IgE antibodies against it, which trigger reactions when you eat mammalian meat or products made from mammals.

What makes AGS unusual among food allergies:
- **It is caused by a tick bite**, not by genetics or early food exposure.
- **It targets a sugar molecule**, not a protein, so it needs its own blood test (alpha-gal IgE) rather than a standard beef or pork allergy panel alone.
- **Reactions are delayed**, usually by 2 to 6 hours ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/signs-symptoms/index.html)). The leading explanation is that alpha-gal in meat travels with fat, which is absorbed slowly ([NIAID workshop report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32057766/)).

For a deeper dive, see [What is Alpha-Gal Syndrome?](/learn/what-is-alpha-gal).

## The diagnosis journey

Many people with AGS figure out what is wrong before their doctors do. The typical experience goes something like this: you start having unexplained digestive problems — cramping, nausea, or diarrhea that comes and goes without a clear pattern. Or you break out in hives in the middle of the night. You visit your doctor, maybe several doctors, and get tested for common GI conditions. Nothing definitive comes back. Eventually, through your own research or a lucky referral, you encounter alpha-gal syndrome, the symptoms match, and a blood test confirms it.

This self-diagnosis path is common in the AGS community, and it is how Shawn, who runs this site, was diagnosed. Part of the reason: in a 2022 CDC survey, 42% of health care providers had never heard of AGS ([MMWR, 2023](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7230a1.htm)). If you brought the diagnosis to your doctor rather than the other way around, you are in good company.

The key test is a blood draw measuring IgE antibodies specific to alpha-gal (Labcorp 650001 or Quest 10554). A positive result has to be read alongside your symptoms; on its own it does not mean you have AGS ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/hcp/diagnosis-testing/index.html)). See our [testing page](/testing) and [symptoms and diagnosis guide](/learn/symptoms-and-diagnosis).

## What you need to avoid

### Mammalian meat
The most obvious category: beef, pork, lamb, goat, venison, bison, rabbit, and any other meat from a mammal. This includes organ meats, processed meats (sausage, hot dogs, deli meat), and meat broths, bouillon, stock, and gravy ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/data-research/products-containing-alpha-gal/index.html)).

### Dairy
This is where AGS gets complicated. Milk and milk products may contain alpha-gal, but CDC notes that many patients with AGS can tolerate them ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/data-research/products-containing-alpha-gal/index.html)). Sensitivity varies enormously. Some people eat dairy freely, some react only to high-fat products like cream, butter, or ice cream, and some react to trace amounts. Your allergist can help you work out where you fall; be cautious while you figure it out.

### Gelatin and hidden ingredients
Gelatin is made from animal collagen and is one of the most common hidden triggers:
- Gel capsule medications and supplements
- Gummy vitamins and candy
- Marshmallows and many desserts
- Some yogurts and sour creams (used as a thickener)

Other hidden mammalian ingredients to watch for: lard, tallow, suet, "natural flavors" (which can be mammal-derived), and glycerin or magnesium stearate in pills. (Carmine, a red dye, comes from insects and does not contain alpha-gal.) Our [label reading guide](/learn/label-reading-and-cross-contamination) goes deeper.

### Medications
This is the area that catches many newly diagnosed patients off guard. Common medication concerns include:
- **Gelatin capsules** — request tablet or vegetarian capsule alternatives from your pharmacist.
- **Heparin** — a blood thinner usually made from pig intestine and used routinely in hospitals. It is on CDC's list of products that may contain alpha-gal, along with pig or cow heart valves, some monoclonal antibodies, and certain antivenoms ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/data-research/products-containing-alpha-gal/index.html)). Make sure it is flagged in your chart.
- **Vaccines** — some contain gelatin. Reactions are very rare, but CDC recommends talking to your provider before a new vaccine or medication ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/managing/index.html)). Don't skip vaccines on your own.
- **Personal care products** — some soaps and lotions contain tallow, collagen, or other animal-derived ingredients. Reactions through the skin are less well studied than reactions to food, so ask your allergist how careful you need to be.

Our [ingredient scanner](/scanner) can help you check products for mammalian-derived ingredients.

## What is safe to eat

The good news is that the list of safe foods is large:
- **Poultry** — chicken, turkey, duck, and other birds ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/data-research/products-containing-alpha-gal/index.html)).
- **Fish and seafood** — these do not contain alpha-gal.
- **Fruits, vegetables, grains, and legumes** — all plant-based foods are safe.
- **Eggs** — safe (they come from birds, not mammals).
- **Plant-based dairy alternatives** — oat milk, almond milk, coconut cream, and similar products are safe substitutes.

Many AGS patients find that their diet actually becomes more varied and adventurous after diagnosis, as they explore cuisines that are naturally low in mammalian ingredients — seafood, poultry-focused dishes, Asian cuisines, and plant-based cooking.

### Everyday swaps

| Instead of… | Try… |
| --- | --- |
| Beef burgers | Ground turkey or chicken, or chickpea, black bean, or mushroom patties |
| Gelatin desserts and gummies | Pectin- or agar-based versions |
| Pork sausage and bacon | Turkey or chicken sausage, or plant-based versions (check for casings) |
| Beef or pork broth | Chicken or vegetable broth |
| Heavy cream and butter (if dairy bothers you) | Oat, coconut, or soy creamers; plant-based butter |
| Lard in baking | Vegetable shortening or oil |

## Building your safety net

### Emergency preparedness
Talk to your allergist about carrying epinephrine; they will help you choose a device ([ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/food/meat/)). Even if your reactions so far have been mild, they can differ from one exposure to the next ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/signs-symptoms/index.html)), and new tick bites may reactivate the allergy.

### Medical identification
In an emergency, you may not be able to tell medical staff about your allergy, and some hospital products, like heparin, may contain alpha-gal. Consider:
- A [medical alert tattoo](/learn/medical-alert-tattoo) on your inner wrist.
- A medical alert bracelet.
- Your phone lock screen displaying your allergy and emergency contact.

Download free medical alert designs from our [downloads page](/downloads).

### Label reading
Reading ingredient labels becomes a daily habit. It is tedious at first but quickly becomes second nature. Key things to check:
- The allergen statement (though alpha-gal is not one of the FDA's major allergens, so it may not be listed)
- Gelatin in any form
- "Natural flavors" — contact the manufacturer if the source is not specified
- Magnesium stearate and other excipients in medications

### Find your community
The single most valuable resource after diagnosis is other AGS patients. Facebook groups dedicated to alpha-gal syndrome are active and knowledgeable — members share safe product lists, doctor recommendations, restaurant experiences, and emotional support. The collective knowledge of the community fills gaps that clinical guidance has not yet caught up to.

### Find the right allergist
Not all allergists are equally familiar with AGS. If your doctor dismisses your symptoms or is unfamiliar with the condition, seek out an allergist who has experience treating alpha-gal patients. The [AAAAI](https://allergist.aaaai.org/find/) and [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/find-an-allergist/) directories can help you locate a specialist.

## Living with AGS long-term

Alpha-gal syndrome changes your daily routine, but most patients adapt well over time. The first few months are the hardest — learning what to avoid, finding safe substitutes, and adjusting to a new way of eating and shopping. After that initial period, the new habits become automatic.

Some things that help with the long-term adjustment:
- **Keep a food diary** during the early months to track what you eat and any symptoms that follow. This helps identify your specific triggers and sensitivity level.
- **Stock your kitchen** with safe staples so you always have something to eat without having to scrutinize labels in the moment.
- **Prepare for social situations** in advance. Family gatherings, work events, and travel all require planning. Eating beforehand, bringing your own food, or choosing restaurants carefully are all common strategies.
- **Prevent new tick bites.** People who avoid new bites may see their sensitivity fade over time, and some can eventually tolerate alpha-gal products again under their allergist's guidance ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/alpha-gal-syndrome/managing/index.html)). See the [prevention guide](/learn/prevention).
- **Stay informed.** Alpha-gal research is moving quickly. Our [research tracker](/research) summarizes new studies and clinical trials.
